Three shells, one core

Pick the case that suits the bike.

The board, screen and battery never change. The shell does: a crisp standard edge, a softer aero profile for the front of a road bike, or a rugged version with a TPU bumper and covered buttons for gravel and mud. All three print without supports.

Seventeen millimetres, back to front.

The board

Routed, checked, ready for JLCPCB.

An ESP32-S3 runs the screen, logging and Wi-Fi. A BL652 handles ANT+ and Bluetooth sensors. A u-blox M10 reads a 12 mm ceramic patch tucked under the top of the lens. Seventy-five parts, all priced and in stock at Mouser or Digi-Key.

Everything else

USB-C, a speaker, and the mount you already have.

USB-C charges the cell at 500 mA from any charger. A small sealed speaker fires through the back for turn prompts and lap alerts. Two side buttons and three soft keys under the screen, each labelled on the display.

Garmin quarter-turn geometry is approximate: check it against a real mount before a long ride.

Lucent interface

Frosted glass, drawn the way the panel will draw it.

This is the reference renderer the firmware ports to LVGL, running live at the panel's real 240 × 320 pixels and quantised to RGB565. Press the keys under the screen, or pick a page.

OPENCYCLE

Colour means something

Everything is calm glass over a deep backdrop until a number matters. Power and heart-rate zones, climb gradient and the route line are the only colours on screen.

No blur at runtime

The soft backdrop is a baked bitmap and glass is a translucent fill with a hairline edge. An ESP32-S3 draws it at full frame rate; no trick here needs a GPU.

Read at a glance

Inter at fixed sizes, figures that never shift width, and the three soft-key labels always in the same place above the keys you press.

Build one

From this repo to your handlebars.

Order the parts and the boards on the same day, and print the case while they ship.

  1. Order the parts

    Mouser and Digi-Key carts with every part number, price and link, plus the battery, lens and small hardware.

    1 h + ~1 week~$89

  2. Order the boards

    Upload the Gerber zip to JLCPCB as it is. Five 4-layer boards and a stencil.

    30 min + 1–2 weeks~$25–40

  3. Print the case

    Oriented STLs for the shells, keys and buttons. No supports.

    ~5 h printing~$1

  4. Assemble the board

    Back side on a hot plate with SAC305, then the front with low-temperature paste.

    3–5 h—

  5. Bring it up

    Rails first, then each chip in turn until all of them answer.

    2 h—

  6. Close it up

    Display, lens, battery and four screws. Then read the step-by-step guides on GitHub.

    1 hInstructions ↗
Where it stands

Designed and checked in software. Not yet built.

Treat the first run of five boards as a prototype. Firmware is not written yet: the interface above is its specification.

Still to prove on a real unit

  • Battery lead polarity against the socket (the board is protected either way)
  • Part rotations in JLCPCB's assembly preview
  • Battery life and backlight brightness in daylight
